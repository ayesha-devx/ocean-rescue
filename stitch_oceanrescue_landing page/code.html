The Ghost Gear Crisis

Lost at Sea.
Never Out of Sight.

Abandoned, lost, and discarded fishing gear (ALDFG) creates perpetual hazards beneath the surface. Synthetic monofilament and heavy industrial nets drift for decades—indiscriminately ensnaring marine life, stripping delicate coral reefs, and degrading into persistent microplastics. Proactive geospatial tracking turns silent aquatic threats into coordinated recovery missions.

Operational Workflow

From Detection to Retrieval

Five coordinated stages ensuring no lost net continues its destructive path.

1
add_location Report Lost Gear

Fishermen, recreational mariners, and divers submit gear type, loss timestamp, GPS coordinates, and optional water photos through our rapid web intake.

2
verified Verify The Report

Authorized NGO teams review incident inputs, confirm sea conditions, eliminate duplicate alerts, and establish initial threat tier ratings.

3
memory Detect and Analyze

Computer vision algorithms analyze drone surveys, satellite passes, and side-scan sonar feeds to classify net density and composition.

4
timeline Forecast and Prioritize

Hydrodynamic ocean drift simulations model probable movement corridors using live tidal, current, and wind feeds to forecast recovery zones.

5
sailing Plan Recovery

Offshore recovery vessels receive waypoint coordinates, bathymetric depth profiles, and safe lift plans to retrieve the gear cleanly.

Hydrodynamic & Weather Ingestion

Surface currents, tidal cycles, and sea-surface temperature data stream directly into models to predict how submerged obstacles shift under rough seas.

Drift Forecasting & Search-Area Bounds

Monte Carlo dispersion calculations estimate gear movement probability radii over 24 to 72 hours, optimizing search radiuses for volunteer and NGO vessels.

Risk-Based Recovery Prioritization

Sorts missions based on ecosystem vulnerability, proximity to shallow coral heads, depth limitations, and seasonal marine mammal migration corridors.
