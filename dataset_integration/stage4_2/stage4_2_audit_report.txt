--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Stage 4.2 establishes the taxonomic and architectural foundation for fusing the
SeaClear (8,610 images, 31,555 annotations) and TrashCan (7,212 images, 12,128
annotations) datasets into a single, unified marine debris benchmark.

Key Achievements:
1. Complete Class Mapping: 100% of categories across SeaClear (40) and TrashCan
   (22) mapped with full backward traceability to original category IDs and names.

   source annotations (SeaClear has 0; TrashCan collapsed cages into unknown).

   in a distinct uncertain class rather than conflating with rope or ROV tether.
5. ROV Equipment & Biology Filtered: 4,280 ROV annotations and 16,595 marine
   organism annotations explicitly isolated and excluded from environmental debris.
6. Path Resolution: Resolved 100% of SeaClear (8,610) and TrashCan (7,212) images
   to canonical disk paths with 0 missing files and 0 name collisions.
7. Critical Data Leakage Discovery: Uncovered that 113 of 115 validation video
   sequences in the official TrashCan release leak into train (98.3% leakage).
   Formulated a zero-leakage Group-by-Video split to eliminate temporal bias.

5. UNRESOLVED MAPPINGS & SCIENTIFIC JUSTIFICATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Status of "trap_cage"
- Examination: Neither SeaClear nor TrashCan provides an actionable `trap_cage` class.
- In SeaClear: 0 trap/pot/cage classes exist among the 40 categories.
- In TrashCan: The author conversion script (`trash_can_coco.py`, line 23) listed
  `'cage'` under `trash_unknown_akas`, collapsing any cage annotations into
  `trash_unknown_instance`.
- Determination: Formally marked as UNSUPPORTED. Fabricating annotations or
  guessing which `unknown_instance` masks are cages without re-annotation would
  introduce severe label noise.

B. Status of "cable_metal" (SeaClear ID 35, 149 annotations)
- Examination: SeaClear annotates 149 instances of `cable_metal`.
- Ambiguity: In underwater imagery, heavy steel wire ropes can represent:
  (1) Active maritime infrastructure (harbor moorings, submarine telecom armor),
  (2) Abandoned trawl warps / commercial fishing winch cables (ALDFG),
  (3) ROV umbilical or tether segments.
- Determination: Kept as a separate class `uncertain_cable_metal` (Target ID 15)
  and excluded from environmental debris metrics until confirmed.

C. Exclusion of ROV Equipment
- Hardware categories: `rov` (TrashCan: 3,317), `rov_cable` (389), `rov_tortuga` (55),
  `rov_vehicle_leg` (461), `rov_bluerov` (58). Total = 4,280 instances.
- Rationale: Survey ROVs capture parts of their own chassis, robotic arms, thrusters,
  and umbilicals in the camera field of view. Training an environmental debris model
  on ROV hardware would cause severe false detections of the inspection vehicle.

7. DATA LEAKAGE AUDIT & ZERO-LEAKAGE SPLIT PLAN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Critical Leakage in Official TrashCan Split:
- TrashCan images are extracted from underwater video clips (`vid_XXXXXX`).
- There are 312 distinct video sequences.
- Official train set contains 310 sequences; val set contains 115 sequences.
- Overlapping sequences: **113 out of 115 validation sequences also exist in train!**
- Impact: 98.3% sequence overlap represents extreme temporal leakage. Adjacent video
  frames from identical camera tracks appear in both train and validation sets,
  rendering published benchmark results severely over-optimistic.

B. Proposed Zero-Leakage Split Strategy:
1. TrashCan Group-by-Video Split:
   - Partition unit: `video_id` (the 312 distinct sequences).
   - Validation sequences: 63 sequences (~20.2%).
   - Training sequences: 249 sequences (~79.8%).
   - Sequence Overlap: **0 sequences (0% leakage)** [PASS].

2. SeaClear Group-by-Deployment Split:
   - Partition unit: Deployment site / camera pairs.
   - Proposed partition:
     - Train Sites: Bistrina (3,652) + Marseille (3,441) = 7,093 images (82.4%).
     - Val Sites  : Lokrum (972) + Jakljan (306) + Slano (239) = 1,517 images (17.6%).
   - Deployment Overlap: **0 images overlapping across sites (0% leakage)** [PASS].
   - Benefit: Evaluates generalizability across completely unseen geographic locations,
     water turbidities, and seabed geologies.
